### 7.1 Electric Road (individual Mastery project)

**Role:** Individual project. Alex's own portfolio page labels it a Mastery Project and prints
"Individual Project" where the five robot pages print a team role under "My Role:" (the outreach
page, 07, prints no role at all) (content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1). The page uses first person for the build: it

**Dates:** None. The portfolio page carries no date, season, or year
(content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1). The résumé gives no date for any Tully Tech

**One line:** A roadway concept that charges electric vehicles as they drive — a solar-powered road
embedded with wireless charging coils — demonstrated with a scaled testing model
(content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1).

#### What the project was

- The stated goal was to develop a roadway capable of charging electric vehicles as they drive
  (content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1).
- The final concept was a solar-powered road embedded with wireless charging coils, enabling cars to
  recharge while in motion (content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1).
- The résumé describes the same project as a prototype solar-powered road that wirelessly charges
  electric cars while they are in motion (content/source/resume/resume-current.png).
- The résumé frames it as a proof of concept and an experiential project, undertaken to apply physics
  principles and engineering techniques (content/source/resume/resume-current.png).
- Alex's private application drafts list the project among his portfolio media pieces, alongside Ball
  Drive and Swerve Drive (content/source/essays/college-essays-private.txt#L3738), and state that his
  school's Mastery program is what enabled both the prosthetic arm and the solar-powered wireless
  charging road (content/source/essays/college-essays-private.txt#L5766-L5767).

#### What was built and what it proved

- Alex built a scaled testing model to demonstrate feasibility
  (content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1).
- The page states the model proved that solar panels could generate sufficient power to wirelessly
  transfer energy to a moving vehicle, including at high speeds
  (content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1).
- The physical model is photographed on the page: a blue mat carrying hand-wound copper coils, a
  small green wireless-charging receiver board, a breadboard with components, and jumper wiring
  (content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1, photo at approximately x 605–930, y 205–355
  of the 980×560 page). This is the only photograph of a physical prototype anywhere in the seven
  portfolio pages (content/source/portfolio/01-monti-robot.jpg through
  content/source/portfolio/07-community-events.jpg).
- No measured number of any kind appears on the page: no power figure, no speed, no efficiency, no
  transfer distance, no coil count in text (content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1).
- No award list and no "Key Innovation(s)" label box appears on this page, unlike pages 1, 3 and 5,
  and no "Of Note" line, unlike page 2 (content/source/portfolio/06-electric-road.jpg#page-1;
  content/source/portfolio/02-swerve-drive.jpg#page-1).

#### Statistics

There are no statistics on this project. Both of its sources are qualitative
