Introduction:
As technology continues to grow and evolve, the world of surveillance closely follows.
With each passing day, surveillance technologies increase, personal privacy decreases, and
individual data about one's identity, location, and person is more exposed. One of the main
factors in the disappearance of personal privacy is the ever-increasing number of security, phone,
or hidden cameras. The number of times someone is caught on camera daily continues to
increase to a point where the average person, not in a city, is on camera around 70 times per day
(KDVR "Caught on Camera," 2018). As surveillance technology evolves, there is almost nothing
that individuals can do to protect their privacy, with the only two viable options for privacy being
isolation or using illegal devices. AntiCam is a device that uses infrared light to visually block a
camera without permanently damaging it. A device that could be helpful to so many, giving back
a sense of privacy and protecting one's image. However, this device can also have unintentional
effects, possibly aiding criminals and those who may use it for nefarious purposes. So the
questions are: do the benefits outweigh the consequences, and is it more dangerous to allow this
technology to be developed and sold by someone who may have more harmful intentions?

Legality and Impact
The idea of camera blockers has existed since the creation of security cameras.
One previously common way to evade security cameras was by going back to hardwire tapping,
where people would physically connect to a nearby cable and change the security feed. The
typical modern way of blocking a security camera is through a camera jammer. Like most
modern devices, cameras transmit data and function based on wifi and cellular signals. Camera
Jammers target the traditional radio wave frequencies that cameras use (between 2.4 GHz and
5.0 GHz) and "disrupt them" by emitting stronger and more prominent signals, effectively
drowning out any signal the camera can produce. This process often overwhelms both the
camera and the network, leading to crashes and system failure.
All existing camera blockers, including the hardwire tap, are incredibly illegal due to the
destruction of property and unpermitted access to private data, especially camera jammers. First,
camera jammers do not block cameras from securing an area; they shut down a camera,
completely nullifying the security of that area and interfering with a private device. They also
have a more likely than not chance of permanently disconnecting or damaging the camera,
adding further destruction of property. However, neither of those is the main issue when it comes
to camera jammers, as discussed earlier, camera jammers work by disrupting wifi and cellular
frequencies, the same frequencies computers use, phones use, emergency calls use, national
security uses, and almost every modern electronic system uses. Disrupting and completely
shutting down these networks creates a risk to the operation of vital health and support systems,
as well as emergency call centers, and poses a significant public safety risk. Due to the use and
very existence of any device that interferes with those frequencies, it is illegal and federally

The Shift from Pre-Existing Technology to AntiCam
Modern camera jammers are illegal and often immoral devices, designed to destroy and
fully disable cameras regardless of what surrounds them, often creating chaos in their wake. Due
to their illegality, camera jammers are designed not to give people personal privacy, but rather to
operate outside the law, aiding in illegal activities. These devices are designed with the exact
opposite intent as AntiCam. AntiCam has been designed to benefit ordinary people and give
them a sense of privacy rather than create chaos. Instead of using radio frequencies to disrupt
wifi, AntiCam emits harmless infrared light in an array. Since most cameras are built to at least
somewhat see in the dark, they need to see a broader range of wavelengths than the typical
human can see, including the lower end of the infrared spectrum. The human eye can not see the
light emitted by AntiCam. However, it can seem like a blinding light to a camera. This means
that instead of disabling a camera, AntiCam creates an area around a person where the camera
cannot see them, allowing them to keep their privacy, without creating any damage. As well as
infrared light to block cameras for the ordinary person, AntiCam has a retro-reflective coating
able to reflect any light directed towards AntiCam back towards its origin. This coating prevents
any flash photography, meaning that any professional camera–especially one that uses flash–
becomes useless in the face of AntiCam. Due to this relatively new technology, its intention is
